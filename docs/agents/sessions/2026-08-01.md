# Session 2026-08-01 — money truth and social tempo

## Product reading

DYOR is a social financial satire about optionality under pressure, not a solitary
spreadsheet and not a contest to equalise bot win percentages. Fairness means that
every seat receives legible information, credible counterplay and more than one
way to recover or exploit a situation. Fun comes from scarcity, negotiation,
memory, overreach and comeback.

## Multiplayer/card decision

Use a hybrid shared table as the default online mode:

- **Market pulses and public crises** are one shared card/effect for the table.
  Players react concurrently; their professions, portfolios and protections make
  the same public event asymmetric.
- **Scarce opportunities** are one visible opportunity contested concurrently.
  Uncontested claims resolve immediately. Contested claims admit at most 2–3
  players into a short deterministic focus/trust tiebreak or a bounded
  fight-or-split negotiation. No full-table auction and no waiting for sequential
  turns.
- **Personal crises and recovery cards** are individual, but expose an immediate
  social hook: ask for help, request a loan, sell/share an asset, or invite a
  partner.
- `Individual` remains an alternate room mode. Pure random private cards are not
  the default because they reduce shared conversation and make luck harder to
  read. Giving every player an identical private choice is also not the default
  because it collapses asymmetry into parallel solitaire.

The existing simultaneous intent window and draft/fight-or-split mechanics are
the implementation base. Card routing must distinguish public state changes from
scarce prizes before draft is promoted to the default loop.

Shared online intent now has a 25-second hard deadline. At expiry, every missing
human intent becomes `pass` in one batch and the round advances. This prevents a
non-active silent player from deadlocking the table. Draft remains experimental:
its current all-`split` branch changes trust but still assigns the card to one
winner, so it is not yet the promised shared ownership outcome.

## Financial invariants accepted today

- Monthly cash change is computed from one ledger:
  active income + passive payouts + asset income − living expenses − upkeep −
  debt service − tax.
- Asset income is taxable.
- Freedom Score annualises **net recurring non-salary cashflow**, not the raw
  `passiveIncome` field. Negative recurring cashflow stays negative.
- Negative settlement drains cash. Bankruptcy begins when cash reaches zero under
  a negative flow, not at an unrelated stress threshold.
- Bankruptcy is a recovery state, not elimination. A player at zero can take bank
  credit, negotiate a player loan, ask for a gift/help, sell or restructure, and
  take a one-time night survival job.
- Asset sales/transfers must atomically update `assets`, `businesses`, and
  `businessSlotsUsed` for both players. Client-supplied staff economics are never
  authoritative.

## Card scope, partnership and preview audit

- The production table does not identify the central card as `для всех`,
  `только вам`, or `один лот на стол`. The header says only `Твой выбор сейчас`,
  and the card badge names its type (`ВОЗМОЖНОСТЬ`, etc.). The production room
  setup also omits the shared/individual/draft explanation that exists in the
  current local working tree, so a player cannot infer card ownership from UI.
- The large consequence chips on the card are not the selected choice. They are
  the first three distinct effects found while scanning all choices. On the
  laundromat card they display the full-buy economics while the selected action
  is equipment lease. This makes the central card contradict the action row.
- The `?` control is press-and-hold/focus only; a normal click closes the preview
  immediately. Its data does call the engine effect registry on a clone, but its
  cashflow snapshot omits asset income/upkeep and marginal tax. An exhaustive
  audit found monthly-preview mismatches on **46 of 149** choices. Example:
  laundromat buy previews `+$600/mo`, while authoritative post-choice cashflow
  changes by `+$1,168/mo` in the controlled test.
- Eight investment choices currently record the same recurring economics both
  in primitive `passive/income/expense` fields and in the added asset. The unified
  settlement then counts both representations. Affected choices include storage,
  AI shop, laundromat, vending, course, coffee franchise, mini-app, and boring
  business fund. This is the main source of the largest preview mismatches.
- Shared-card `partnership.invite` has a coherent group resolver for only two
  cards (`opp-storage`, `opp-route`): six contributors become one six-player
  partnership with 1/6 rules and normalized cost/value. Representation is still
  mirrored as one stake asset per player, and rounding produced `$402` total
  income for a `$400` asset.
- The generic two-person offer builder does not protect the same opportunity.
  Replaying one coffee card as A-B 50/50 and then A-C 50/50 minted two identical
  asset ids for A and doubled the single coffee shop's total value/income. There
  is no canonical lot with a 100% share cap or atomic group cap table.
- The interest banner says up to three players proceed, but pressing interest
  immediately calls both `expressInterest()` and `passInterest()`, closing the
  window before the advertised timer. Any alive player is allowed to close the
  window, and the offer builder falls back to the first other player when no
  selected partner exists. Scarcity is therefore copy, not a completed rule.
- Static card audit: **66 cards / 149 choices**, all have non-empty Russian title,
  text and localized choice labels; no card is structurally empty and no used
  effect type is unregistered. One non-pass action (`prot-accountant:later`) has
  an empty effect array. Structural completeness does not imply truthful play.
- Three cards have no direct economic effect: Crypto Winter and Volatility Spike
  only add stress despite claiming a market event; Business Partnership adds
  trust but creates no online deal. The P2P loan is worse online: the lender loses
  `$1K/$2K`, while no borrower receives cash and no liability or contract appears.
- Most visible opportunity descriptions are dry setup; much of the attempted joke
  lives in `hostCue`, which is silent for ordinary opportunities because host
  interjections are gated by type and cooldown. Several lines are literal English
  calques or repeated memes. Content QA must separately score language quality,
  decision tension, and mechanical truth instead of treating localization presence
  as a humor/passability result.

## Implemented BASIC / PRO contract

- The earlier hybrid scarce-lot proposal was rejected as too heavy for a
  15-minute phone game. The approved default is now **BASIC**: one private card
  per player, simultaneous decisions, one small public market effect, and no
  cap-table builder in the first-session loop.
- **PRO** is an explicit room mode. It preserves the shared card, partnerships,
  percentage deals and advanced controls instead of exposing them as mandatory
  first-turn complexity.
- A BASIC card is labeled `ЛИЧНАЯ · …`; the phase says
  `Личная карта · только вам`. The shared macro card is a separate compact
  `РЫНОК · ВСЕМ` strip. PRO-only partnership choices are labeled and disabled.
- From round 3 an opportunity/modern-earning card can be offered to exactly one
  player. Acceptance replaces the recipient's private card for that month,
  locks the finder to pass and pays the finder 5% of the accepted upfront cost.
  A bot accepts immediately in offline play so no phone user waits on ceremony.
- Recipient-specific server snapshots include only the viewer's private card and
  relevant personal offers. They expose the ids of players who have locked in,
  but redact every other player's actual intent until resolution.
- The `?` control now toggles on click and previews the selected choice. Preview
  uses the same full monthly ledger as settlement. Exhaustive regression covers
  all 149 card choices and verifies preview monthly deltas against authoritative
  resolution.
- Duplicate recurring effects were removed from asset acquisitions. An asset's
  income and upkeep now live in the asset ledger once instead of also being
  mirrored as primitive passive/income/expense fields.

## Process observations for Studio

- Balance simulations are useful only after authority and ledger invariants pass.
  The exhaustive choice-level preview/resolution test found actionable math truth;
  aggregate win percentages would not have detected duplicated asset income.
- Recipient-private state makes whole-snapshot hashes a false divergence signal.
  Observability must distinguish public consensus from intentionally private
  projections.
- Large DOM snapshots and full Vite asset listings are major context consumers.
  After one orientation snapshot, use scoped state probes and report only summary
  counts/hash/errors.
- Context packs should prioritize current task, accepted decisions, dirty files
  and last verification delta. The long historical session narrative is useful
  as an audit trail but should not be injected wholesale into every run.

## Follow-up: ownership, onboarding and mobile transport

- The fixed 5% finder fee was removed. In BASIC the owner of an opportunity may
  keep it, pass and burn it, offer it directly to one player for any non-negative
  integer price, or list it to the table at an asking price. Direct rejection
  leaves the card with the seller; acceptance transfers the asking price and
  locks the seller to Pass. A table listing immediately sacrifices the seller's
  move and the first consenting buyer gets the one card.
- Bots do not accept every price. Their deterministic check uses the authoritative
  choice preview, affordability and remaining match horizon. The human price has
  no balance cap; `$1,000,000` is only a payload-abuse ceiling.
- First-run learning now freezes the local timer and, online, the authoritative
  room timer and bot cascade. The table resumes only after every learning human
  finishes or explicitly skips. A disconnected learner gets a 30-second reconnect
  grace before the pause is released.
- The players step is a real interaction: the spotlight rail remains tappable,
  the actual profile sheet opens above the coach mark, and four reactions can be
  sent. The coach mark returns to the same step after the sheet closes.
- Telegram's `safeAreaInset` and `contentSafeAreaInset`, their CSS variables and
  change events now feed the game safe-area tokens. The round/timer is centered,
  phase sits on its own row, and there are no game buttons under Telegram's
  floating Close or ellipsis controls. Rules and settings remain in the bottom
  action menu. Settings has a prominent `Вернуться в игру`; leave/surrender are
  separated under `ОПАСНЫЕ ДЕЙСТВИЯ`.
- The reported long-session network failure had a concrete client cause:
  `wsClient.send()` silently discarded every message after a socket close and no
  reconnect existed. The client now retries with backoff, replays only the safe
  room binding (never ambiguous financial commands), restores the latest server
  snapshot and exposes a centered reconnect banner. A per-WebView resume token
  lets the server replace a stale socket without allowing another tab to steal a
  seat.
- Reproducible `tools/network-lab/reconnect-smoke.mjs` now covers reconnect,
  tutorial command blocking/unblocking and targeted reactions. Live local smoke
  passed in room `SYPB5`.

## Follow-up process observations for Studio

- Transport health is product state. Studio should track `connected`,
  `reconnecting`, room rebind and last authoritative snapshot separately; a live
  React screen is not proof of a live server channel.
- Do not retry financial commands automatically after an ambiguous disconnect:
  the server may already have applied them. Reconnect the identity, fetch state,
  then let the player decide from the authoritative snapshot.
- Token/context waste observed again: Vite's full asset-size listing is thousands
  of low-value tokens. Future build wrappers should retain exit code, warnings and
  the final JS/CSS totals while storing the full listing in a log file outside the
  agent context.
- UI validation should use one screenshot per materially different state, then
  DOM probes for timer, round, hit targets, safe-area position and visibility.
  Repeating full-page screenshots adds less evidence than small invariant probes.

## Mobile card stage and lobby hierarchy

- The active turn now behaves like a small stage rather than a long dashboard.
  While a card is being dealt the bottom action dock stays out of the way; the
  card then settles, its semantic signal and economics appear in a short stagger,
  and the decision dock enters last. Any pointer press skips the wait. Reduced
  motion bypasses the sequence entirely; no typewriter animation delays reading.
- Every card gets a compact narrative signal based on its real type: opportunity,
  work, crisis, market, protection, expense, life event and so on. It gives the
  player a reason to care before the raw numbers without changing engine math.
- During an active decision the opponent rail, shared market and self HUD become
  denser, while cash, monthly flow, expenses and risk remain visible. Inventory
  moves behind the existing additional-actions control. This recovers enough
  vertical space for the entire card and its consequences.
- Internal card scrolling was removed. A 25-round DOM-driven run at 402×874 found
  `clientHeight === scrollHeight` for all 25 card bodies and all decision buttons
  remained clickable. A separate 375×812 run and an 874×402 landscape run also
  fit without horizontal or internal overflow. A public 1280×720 probe then caught
  a separate short-wide clipping case; its compact rule now hides the redundant
  ledger, keeps desktop-scale copy and restores exact card height parity without
  switching to the tiny phone-landscape typography.
- Native Xcode Simulator was checked first but is not installed on this Mac
  (`simctl` is unavailable). Device QA therefore used exact iPhone 16 Pro CSS
  metrics in Chrome DevTools, plus the narrower phone and landscape stress cases.
- The waiting-room screen now has a clear order: room/code status, seats, bot
  control, one collapsed match-settings summary, then sticky exit/start actions.
  Expanding settings reveals the existing mode, length and privacy controls; no
  functionality was removed. The reactions button is lifted clear of the sticky
  action zone.

## Process observations from the visual pass

- The efficient loop was: one baseline screenshot, scoped DOM geometry, a small
  layout change, then a many-card invariant run. It produced stronger evidence
  than repeatedly reading full screenshots or running balance simulations for a
  layout problem.
- Keep art/motion questions separate from economy authority. Card staging can
  improve comprehension, but every displayed consequence must still come from
  the engine preview/ledger path.
- Persist numeric evidence rather than raw dumps: viewport, card client/scroll
  heights, overflow mode, round/hash/error counts. Full DOM snapshots, complete
  card catalogs and Vite's asset listing are large context costs with little
  incremental value.
- Reuse one live browser session and the six-socket harness across checks. New
  sessions should consume this compact evidence instead of replaying the whole
  historical investigation.
